Make the part the starting point.

Give the part a name and describe what you want to achieve. A simple description is useful: finish two opposing faces, improve an existing process, or discuss a new application.

You do not need to select a machine first. The brief should explain the work before it proposes the equipment.

Separate knowns from open questions.

Include material and condition, outside dimensions, finished thickness and any surface requirements available on the drawing.

  • Identify the locating features and faces to be ground.
  • Keep size, geometric requirements and surface finish in their original units.
  • Leave uncertain values open; do not substitute a reference case’s specifications.

Explain the work around the part.

Production rate, part mix, changeover and loading needs help frame the process discussion. Add the project stage and timing, even if they are still being defined.

For existing equipment, include the make, model and current controls. Describe the issue or change you want to address.

Keep one clear version of the request.

Review the application, technical information, contact details and open questions together. Your saved inquiry remains with the project, ready for C&B to review.

An inquiry does not confirm process feasibility, machine selection or a price. Those decisions follow the engineering and commercial discussion.

Build your application brief.

Work through the details you know. Keep the rest visible as questions for engineering.
